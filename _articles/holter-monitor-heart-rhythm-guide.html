Holter Monitor Test: Quick Patient Guide

Medically reviewed by Dr. Gerald Thang | Updated August 2026

Quick Answer: A Holter monitor is a continuous 24 to 48-hour ambulatory ECG test. While a standard clinic ECG only records 10 seconds, a Holter captures intermittent palpitations, dizziness, or Atrial Fibrillation during your normal daily activities.

Why Use a Patch Holter vs. Traditional Wires?

  • No Cables: A single, lightweight chest patch replaces messy hanging lead wires.
  • Invisible: Fits discreetly under your normal daily clothing.
  • Better Signal: Reduces wire movement interference, producing cleaner ECG readings.
  • Comfortable Sleep: Enables sleep in any position without pulling leads.

How to Prepare for Fitting

  • Shower before fitting: You must keep the monitor dry during the test.
  • Skip lotions/oils: Avoid chest moisturizers so the adhesive stays attached.
  • Wear loose clothes: Button-up shirts make placement quick and simple.
  • Chest hair: Clinic staff may trim a small patch for proper electrode stick.

What Doctors Look For

Parameter What It Evaluates
Heart Rate Range Tracks continuous low, average, and high heart rates day and night.
Ectopic Burden Measures the percentage of premature (extra) heartbeats like PVCs or PACs.
AFib Detection Identifies short, hidden spells of Atrial Fibrillation to manage stroke risk.
