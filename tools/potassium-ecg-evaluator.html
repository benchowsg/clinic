Understanding Your Body's Potassium Balance

How a simple blood mineral powers your muscle contraction, heart rhythm, and nerve signaling.

What Happens When Potassium Drops Too Low?

When potassium falls below 3.5 mmol/L (often due to intense sweating, water pills, or gastrointestinal fluid loss), your heart muscle cells take much longer to recharge between beats. You might experience muscle cramps (especially in your calves), leg weakness, fatigue, or feelings of skipping a beat or fluttering.

What Happens When Potassium Gets Too High?

If potassium builds up above 5.5 mmol/L (common in kidney conditions or with certain blood pressure medications), it over-excites your heart's electrical system, slowing down the pulse signal. High potassium often causes no noticeable symptoms until it reaches critical levels, making routine blood screening vital.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions patients ask about potassium blood tests and heart health.

Singapore clinical laboratories (such as SGH, NUH, SingHealth, and private diagnostic centers) use mmol/L (millimoles per liter) as standard SI units for electrolyte reporting. 1 mmol/L of potassium is identical to 1 mEq/L used in some international references.

While dietary foods like bananas, spinach, and coconut water provide potassium, severe drops (below 3.0 mmol/L) or chronic imbalances require a clinical review to identify the underlying cause (such as medication side effects or kidney losses) before taking high-dose supplements.

Certain blood pressure medications (like ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or spironolactone) protect your heart and blood vessels by helping your body release excess salt. In doing so, they can cause your kidneys to retain potassium, which is why routine monitoring is recommended.

If your health check report shows potassium levels outside 3.5 – 5.0 mmol/L, or if you experience unexplained muscle weakness, fatigue, or heart palpitations, scheduling a medical assessment ensures your medications, organ functions, and cardiovascular rhythm are safely aligned.
