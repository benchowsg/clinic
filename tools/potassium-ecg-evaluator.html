What Happens When Potassium Drops Too Low?

When your potassium drops below 3.5 mmol/L (often from intense sweating, water pills, or stomach flus), your heart muscle cells have to work much harder to reset between beats. You might experience muscle cramps, weakness, sudden fatigue, or a feeling that your heart is skipping a beat or fluttering.

What Happens When Potassium Gets Too High?

If potassium builds up above 5.5 mmol/L (common in kidney conditions or with certain blood pressure medications), it over-excites your heart's electrical system. This causes the heartbeat signal to stretch out and slow down. Surprisingly, high potassium often has no noticeable symptoms until it reaches dangerous levels, making blood tests essential.

Medical Sources & Local Clinical Standards

The ranges and heart trace patterns shown in this educational tool are based on established clinical guidelines used across Singapore clinics and hospitals:

  • MOH Agency for Care Effectiveness (ACE) Guidelines: National Singapore clinical guidance for managing blood mineral balances, medication safety, and kidney checks.
  • Singapore Laboratory Reference Standards: Standardized reporting formats used by SGH, NUH, and local diagnostic centers where 3.5 to 5.0 mmol/L is recognized as the healthy baseline.
  • European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Clinical Guidance: World-recognized diagnostic standards mapping blood chemistry levels to heart monitor patterns.
