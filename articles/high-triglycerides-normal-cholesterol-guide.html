The Forgotten Blood Fat: Why High Triglycerides Silently Strain Your Heart & Liver

Triglycerides are not caused by fatty foods alone. Discover how hidden sugars, refined hawker carbs, and insulin resistance drive hypertriglyceridemia in Singapore.

Written & Reviewed by Dr. Gerald Thang

Principal Physician

MBBS (Singapore), MRCP (UK), Graduate Diploma in Cardiology (Singapore)

⚡ Quick Summary: Hypertriglyceridemia Insights

  • Sugar to Fat Conversion: Triglycerides are unused energy stored in blood. The liver rapidly converts excess refined carbohydrates, fructose, and alcohol into triglycerides.
  • The TG-to-HDL Ratio: A high Triglyceride-to-HDL ratio indicates insulin resistance and small, dense LDL particles that easily embed into arterial walls.
  • Fatty Liver & Pancreatitis Risk: High triglycerides drive fat accumulation in liver cells (MASLD) and, at severe levels (> 5.6 mmol/L), can trigger acute pancreatitis.
  • Reversible Condition: Cutting liquid sugars, substituting refined carbs with whole grains, and increasing aerobic physical activity can rapidly reduce triglyceride levels.

When patients review their lipid panel after a routine health screening in Singapore, their attention almost always jumps straight to LDL Cholesterol ("bad cholesterol"). However, there is another critical blood lipid that is frequently overlooked: Triglycerides.

While cholesterol is used by the body to build cell membranes and hormones, triglycerides represent unused calories stored as fat in the bloodstream. Having high triglycerides—a condition known medically as Hypertriglyceridemia—is a major hallmark of underlying insulin resistance, fatty liver disease, and heightened cardiovascular risk.
